# Lucidpeak Design Context

This file captures the design context for lucidpeak.co. Any skill that produces visuals, motion, or copy must consult this before making assumptions.

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary audience**: fellow indie builders and developers — people browsing small-studio portfolios for inspiration, reassurance that you can ship small focused things, or to size up the studio's taste before reaching out.

**Context of use**: visiting casually on a desktop browser, often between tabs. Low-stakes exploration, not a conversion funnel. They spend more time with something that rewards a closer look and leave immediately if it smells generic.

**Job to be done**: form an opinion about the studio in under 10 seconds — "do these people make things I'd respect?" — then stay longer if the answer is yes. Each app window is effectively a mini case study that demonstrates craft by how it feels, not what it claims.

### Brand Personality

**Three words**: patient, tactile, opinionated.

- **Patient**: no urgency timers, no "sign up today" pressure, no frantic motion. Things unfold at their own pace.
- **Tactile**: the macOS-window metaphor, the cream paper palette, the soft-card shadows — every surface should feel like it could be picked up. Warmth comes from texture, not from illustrations of hands or heart emoji.
- **Opinionated**: strong taste expressed by what is *not* there. Refusal of SaaS clichés IS the brand. We'd rather make one unexpected choice than five safe ones.

**Emotional goals**: quiet confidence; the small smile of recognizing something made by hand; the urge to try the product, not just read about it.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme**: light-only. Enforced in `app/layout.tsx` with `colorScheme: "only light"` — no dark-mode toggle. Cream paper world, lit from above.

**Palette** (already in `app/globals.css`):
- Background `#eceae4` (warm cream)
- Card surface `#f6f4ee` (slightly brighter cream)
- Input/hover surface `#fbf8f2` (brightest)
- Text: zinc scale (`#18181b` → `rgba(63, 63, 70, 0.55)`)
- Per-app accents held in `content/apps.ts`: Lettermatch gold `#e5a853`, IssueAggregator lavender `#7c8ef0`, hiRelay teal `#5ed4b3`, BuildMeThis rose `#ef6f8c`
- Supporting: emerald `#10b981` for live status, warm browns `#b5812f` for deepened gold
- **No pure black, no pure white**

**Typography**: currently Geist Sans + Geist Mono (Vercel defaults, Next-bundled). Acceptable for now — not on the reject list — but on the edge of monoculture. When adding new type, reach for distinctive display faces (not Fraunces, Cormorant, Syne, IBM Plex, Instrument, Outfit, DM Sans/Serif, Plus Jakarta, Playfair, Newsreader, Lora, Space Mono/Grotesk). Consider Pangram Pangram, Klim, ABC Dinamo, Velvetyne for a display pair if the brief ever calls for it.

**Signature device**: a macOS-like desktop frame (`.mac-screen`, `.mac-display`, `.mac-desktop`) with stacked draggable windows, traffic-light buttons, and a dock. Code-typing animations inside WIP windows. A rotating conic "focus halo" around the active window. This metaphor IS the differentiation — treat it as the primary brand asset, not a gimmick to strip later.

**Motion philosophy**: motion is decorative priority per the accessibility choice — but still disciplined. Exponential easing (`cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1)` is the house curve, used in window transitions). No bounce. Animations tell small stories (typing, matching, live beacon) rather than decorating elements.

**Anti-references** (explicit):
- Generic SaaS hero with headline + logo cloud + gradient CTA
- Cyan-on-dark "developer aesthetic"
- Purple-to-blue headline gradients
- Rounded-rectangle feature cards in a 3-up grid
- Neon glowing accents on dark backgrounds
- Any variation of "border-left: 4px solid accent" as a design touch

### Design Principles

Derived from the conversation. Every design decision on this site should pass these:

1. **Show, don't market.** Each project window should demonstrate the product's personality through motion and layout, not through feature bullets or a CTA. The preview *is* the pitch.
2. **Warmth through texture, not illustration.** Paper, ruled lines, tiny type stamps, cream gradients — never emoji, never "friendly" cartoon illustrations, never rounded-corner icon-above-heading templates.
3. **Refuse the SaaS playbook.** Whenever a decision has a "safe SaaS answer," deliberately pick the other one. Light theme, quiet accent use, asymmetric composition, no hero-metric numbers.
4. **Every element earns its place.** Lean toward removing rather than adding. If a label or icon or shadow doesn't change the reader's understanding, cut it.
5. **Motion is a narrator, not decoration.** An animation should tell you something about the product you couldn't learn from a static frame (e.g., "Lettermatch compares two watchlists and finds the overlap"). If it's just a hover wiggle, remove it.
